Selected work

A network foundation two clouds and a datacentre share

Every team solved connectivity its own way, and there was no highly available path between Google Cloud, on-premise and the second public cloud.

I led the build of a highly available Cloud Interconnect foundation with geo-redundant failover, and a pattern that lets spoke networks attach to it without bespoke work. I own its infrastructure as code and the cloud-side configuration; the architecture was co-designed with one other engineer, whom I mentored through it.

It runs in production today, carrying live workloads — inference, containers, serverless — with automatic failover across regions, headroom to scale, and a measurable latency improvement on the traffic it already carries.

Egress policy that runs inside the request, not beside it

Traffic arriving over Private Service Connect cannot be attributed to the consumer behind it, so egress policy could not be enforced per consumer. I proved the managed options could not do it before building anything.

I designed and built a control plane that identifies each connection's consumer, resolves its place in the resource hierarchy, and evaluates allow-or-deny in-process inside the proxy — no network round trip on the decision path, fail-closed by default. I exposed it to other teams as a Terraform provider I wrote, so adopting it looks like adopting any other resource.

Live in development and production on a two-region Kubernetes deployment, run hands-off with GitOps and metric-driven autoscaling.

One path for every new project

New cloud projects, their Terraform workspaces and their infrastructure repositories were created ad hoc, with no consistent folder or labelling model.

I designed the automated path they all take now — project creation, labelling, perimeter placement, workload identity federation, multi-environment support, group membership — and the API in front of it.

Every new project goes through it. Hundreds of applications have been provisioned on one uniform model, where before there was no consistent structure to inherit.
